===============================================================
  1. THE MARKET YOU ARE IN
===============================================================

  You are in CMMS — Computerized Maintenance Management
  Systems. It is a real, growing, well-funded category.

  Global market size, 2026:        USD 1.45 – 2.4 billion
  Forecast by 2035/36:             USD 3.5 – 5.9 billion
  Growth rate:                     9 – 11% a year

  The estimates vary by research house because they draw
  the category boundary differently, but every single one
  agrees on direction and pace. This is a market growing
  at roughly 10% annually for the next decade.

  More useful to you: Asia-Pacific is the fastest-growing
  region, and the analysts specifically note "mobile-first,
  cloud bypass" as the regional pattern — plants that never
  had a desktop CMMS going straight to phones.

  That is exactly what you built. You are not early and you
  are not late. You are on time, in the right region, with
  the right form factor.

  The market is also described consistently as
  "fragmented" — no single player owns it. Good news for a
  newcomer: you are not trying to displace a monopoly.

===============================================================
  3. HOW YOUR FEATURES ACTUALLY STAND
===============================================================

  I went through this carefully rather than taking your
  marketing page at face value.

  WHERE YOU MATCH THE LEADERS

  Work orders, PM scheduling, asset register, QR scanning,
  spares and inventory, purchase orders, checklists,
  reporting, audit trail, mobile, offline queue, roles and
  permissions, REST API.

  All present, all standard. No gaps against MaintainX or
  Limble on the fundamentals. This is table stakes and you
  have cleared it.

  WHERE YOU ARE GENUINELY AHEAD

  1. AI ANSWERS FROM THE CUSTOMER'S OWN MANUALS, CITED

     This is your strongest card and I want to be precise
     about why.

     The competition's AI is mostly: generate a procedure
     from a manual (MaintainX), suggest PM tasks from a
     manual (Limble), predict failures from history
     (Fiix Foresight), or a chat assistant over their own
     knowledge base.

     Yours indexes the customer's actual equipment
     documentation and answers a plain-language fault
     question with the manual name and page cited. That is
     retrieval-augmented generation done on the customer's
     private corpus. Limble and MaintainX are moving this
     way but neither ships exactly this today.

     The citation is the part that matters commercially. An
     AI answer without a source is a liability in an
     industrial setting — nobody will act on it at 3am.
     An answer that says "Atlas Copco GA-75 manual, section
     4.3" is something a technician will actually follow.

  2. ACOUSTIC CONDITION MONITORING FROM A PHONE

     Everyone else does condition monitoring with sensors.
     Limble integrates Monnit, Augury, AssetWatch. eMaint
     uses Fluke hardware. Fiix uses Rockwell OT.

     All of those require buying hardware per machine.
     For a Pakistani plant that is often the deal-breaker —
     the software is affordable, the sensor rollout is not.

     Yours records 30 seconds on a phone the technician
     already owns and compares against a baseline. No
     hardware, no capex, no procurement cycle.

     Is it as accurate as a proper vibration sensor? No,
     and you should never claim it is. But "free and
     approximately right on every machine" beats "precise
     on the four machines we could afford sensors for."
     That is a real argument and you should make it exactly
     that way.

  3. URDU VOICE INPUT

     I could not find a single competitor offering Urdu.
     Cryotos and Maintastic do multilingual voice-to-ticket,
     which is the closest, but not Urdu specifically.

     This solves the single biggest reason maintenance
     records stay empty: the fitter will not type on a
     phone with oily hands, and will not type in English at
     all. Your own marketing says this and it is correct.

     A foreign vendor will not build this. The market is
     too small for them and too specific. It is defensible
     for exactly as long as you are the only one who cares
     about it.

  4. FLAT PRICING INSTEAD OF PER-SEAT

     Per-user pricing actively punishes the customer for
     putting the system in more hands — which is the thing
     that makes a CMMS work. Every plant using MaintainX
     rations logins.

     You charge per plant. A 25-person team costs the same
     as a 5-person team. This is a better model for your
     market and worth saying out loud on the pricing page.

  WHERE YOU ARE BEHIND, HONESTLY

  1. NO INTEGRATIONS. MaintainX lists NetSuite, SAP,
     Dynamics, Slack, QuickBooks, Jira, Power BI, Samsara,
     Zapier. You have a REST API and nothing pre-built.
     For SMEs this rarely kills a deal. For anyone with SAP
     it kills it immediately.

  2. NO IOT SENSOR INPUT. Your condition monitoring is
     phone-only. A plant that already owns sensors cannot
     feed them in. Worth adding eventually as an upgrade
     path, not urgent.

  3. NO THIRD-PARTY REVIEWS. MaintainX has 1,540 G2
     reviews at 4.8. Limble has 685. You have zero, on
     any platform. For a buyer comparing options online
     you are invisible — not badly rated, literally absent
     from the comparison.

  4. NO PROVEN SCALE. Nobody can point to your system
     running a 200-asset plant for two years. Every
     competitor can. This is the thing founding customers
     buy you.

  5. SINGLE-PLANT SUBSCRIPTIONS. Multi-site is handled by
     "talk to us". Competitors sell multi-site as a tier.
     Fine for now, a ceiling later.

  6. NO SOC 2 OR ISO 27001. Does not matter for an SME in
     Karachi. Matters enormously the day a multinational's
     procurement department gets involved.

  4. WHAT YOUR SITES LOOK LIKE — HONEST ASSESSMENT
===============================================================

  I read the marketing site as a buyer would.

  THE WRITING IS THE BEST THING ABOUT IT

  Genuinely. "The maintenance app that reads your manuals
  and answers back" is a better headline than anything on
  MaintainX or Limble's homepage. The bearing-seizure
  comparison on the pricing page — Rs 200,000 failure
  versus Rs 24,000 a month — is exactly how industrial
  software should be sold.

  The compressor example with the real Atlas Copco citation
  is the most persuasive thing on the site. Most SaaS sites
  would have used a fake screenshot.

  Speed is good: homepage 22 KB, loads in 0.19 seconds. The
  whole customer app is 148 KB. MaintainX's marketing site
  is several megabytes. Yours will load on a 3G connection
  in a plant. That matters here.

  Responsive design is properly done — 14 media queries on
  the site, 11 in the app.

  WHAT IS MISSING, AND IT IS COSTING YOU

  a) NO SOCIAL PREVIEW TAGS. I checked: no og:title, no
     og:image, no og:description, no twitter:card.

     This is the one to fix first. When you send
     hsbfix.org on WhatsApp — which is your main sales
     channel — the recipient sees a bare grey link with no
     picture, no headline, no description. It looks like
     something a stranger forwarded. With og tags it shows
     your hero image, your headline and a line of copy.

     WhatsApp is how you sell. Your link looks broken on
     WhatsApp. Two hours of work, biggest single return
     of anything on this list.

  b) NO FAVICON. The browser tab shows a blank page icon.
     Small thing, reads as unfinished.

  c) NO SCHEMA MARKUP, NO CANONICAL. Hurts search ranking
     and stops Google showing rich results for your
     pricing.

  d) NO SOCIAL PROOF ANYWHERE. I searched the homepage for
     "customer", "client", "trusted", "case study",
     "testimonial" — zero matches. No logos, no names, no
     quotes, no numbers.

     Understandable when you have no customers. But it is
     the first thing a plant manager looks for, and the
     founding-customer offer is the right answer to it.
     Get three names on that page as fast as you can.

  e) THE ADMIN PANEL IS INVISIBLE TO BUYERS. Fine — it
     should be. But when you sell, the buyer needs to see
     it. Record a five-minute walkthrough.

  THE APP ITSELF

  Clean, fast, sensible login. Says plainly "Accounts are
  created by HSB Fix Services" which is correct for your
  invite-only model.

  One observation: a prospect who lands on app.hsbfix.org
  can do absolutely nothing. No demo, no screenshots, no
  way in. Consider a read-only demo account with a sample
  plant loaded — "see it working" converts far better
  than "request access". This is the single biggest
  conversion gap in the funnel.


===============================================================
  5. YOUR PRICING — WHAT TO CHANGE
===============================================================

  Current:
    Essential      Rs 12,000/mo   10 users, 1 plant, 10 GB
    Professional   Rs 24,000/mo   25 users, 1 plant, 30 GB
    Enterprise     quoted
    Trial          7 days
    Founding       Rs 12,000/mo for year one, 3 places

  WHAT IS RIGHT

  The two-tier split is correct, and putting AI and
  condition monitoring behind Professional is exactly the
  right line — those are the features that change outcomes,
  and the Rs 12,000 gap is justified by one avoided
  breakdown. Your own pricing page argues this well.

  Flat per-plant pricing is right. Do not switch to
  per-seat.

  The founding-customer offer is well constructed: real
  discount, real deadline, and you ask for a testimonial
  in return. That is how you solve the no-social-proof
  problem. Keep it.

  WHAT I WOULD CHANGE

  1. ESSENTIAL IS UNDERPRICED. At Rs 12,000 you are at
     $43/month for a whole plant. EZO charges $14 per user
     — three users and they have passed you.

     You are not competing on price with anyone. You have
     already won on price by a factor of ten. Pricing
     lower does not win more deals, it just signals the
     product is small.

     Move Essential to Rs 15,000 – 18,000. You will not
     lose a single sale. Anyone who balks at Rs 18,000 was
     never going to pay Rs 12,000 either — that is a
     budget problem, not a price problem.

  2. THE 7-DAY TRIAL IS TOO SHORT. Industry standard is
     14 – 30 days. Click Maint and Limble both give 14.

     More importantly: a maintenance system cannot prove
     itself in 7 days. A PM cycle has not come round. No
     breakdown has happened. The AI has not been tested on
     a real fault because no real fault occurred that week.

     You are asking them to judge a product before it has
     had a chance to work. Go to 14 days minimum. 30 is
     better for your first ten customers — the cost to you
     is near zero and the conversion difference is large.

  3. ADD A SMALL TIER. You already say in your FAQ that
     you will find a price for a three-person workshop.
     Make it a real plan: Rs 6,000 – 7,000, 5 users, 5 GB,
     no AI. It captures the small workshops, gets your
     name into more plants, and several will grow into
     Essential.

  4. CHARGE A SETUP FEE ON ENTERPRISE. You currently give
     setup, import and training free on every tier. Fiix
     charges $1,500 – $10,000 for implementation packages.
     Free setup is a great closing argument on Essential
     and Professional — keep it there. But on Enterprise,
     where you are travelling to site and training shifts,
     charge Rs 100,000 – 200,000. It costs you real days.

  5. RAISE PRICES FOR CUSTOMERS 11 ONWARDS. Lock your
     first ten in at today's rates for two years and say
     so — it is a strong reason to sign now. Then move
     Professional to Rs 30,000 – 35,000. You will still be
     a tenth of MaintainX.

  A NOTE ON CURRENCY

  Your costs are partly in dollars — Supabase, Gemini,
  Resend — and your revenue is entirely in rupees. The
  rupee has been stable around 277 for a year, but if it
  moves you are exposed.

  Two protections: price in rupees but review annually,
  and if you ever sell to a Gulf customer, price that
  contract in dollars.


===============================================================
  6. WHAT THE BUSINESS IS WORTH
===============================================================

  This is the part you asked about most directly, so I will
  be blunt.

  Software businesses are valued on REVENUE, not on how
  good the code is. The 2026 benchmarks:

    Micro-SaaS under $1m ARR    2.5 – 4x ARR
                                (or 4 – 6x annual profit)
    Acquire.com median          3.9x profit
    Flippa small SaaS           2.5 – 4.5x profit
    Pre-revenue                 essentially code value only

  That last line is where you are today. Sources are
  consistent and brutal on this: a pre-revenue SaaS product
  sells for the value of its code and nothing else, often
  a few thousand dollars, because the buyer is taking all
  the market risk.

  So there are two honest numbers.

  A) WHAT IT WOULD COST TO REBUILD (asset value)

  This is the floor, and the number to use if you sell the
  code itself rather than a business.

    81 database tables
    99 database functions
    98 row-level security policies
    13 edge functions
    3 front-end applications
    19 feature modules
    ~1.3 MB of hand-written source

  At Pakistani senior developer rates — Rs 1,600 – 1,800
  per hour, or $25 – 75 for an agency — a competent team
  rebuilding this from a written specification would need
  roughly 900 – 1,400 hours. Call it six to nine months
  of one strong developer, longer in practice because the
  business rules had to be discovered, not just typed.

    Rebuild cost, local rates      Rs 1.8 – 3.5 million
    Rebuild cost, agency rates     Rs 4 – 8 million

  But rebuild cost is not sale value. A buyer pays for
  what it earns, not what it cost. Treat Rs 2.5 – 4 million
  as a realistic asking price for the code and brand today,
  to a buyer who already has customers to sell it to — a
  Pakistani industrial automation firm, an ERP vendor
  wanting a maintenance module, or an engineering services
  company.

  B) WHAT IT IS WORTH WITH CUSTOMERS (business value)

  This is where the real money is, and the arithmetic is
  worth internalising.

  Assume Professional at Rs 24,000, mostly annual billing,
  and roughly 75% margin after Supabase, Gemini and Resend.

    10 plants    Rs 2.88 m/yr revenue   ~Rs 2.2 m profit
                 Value at 3-4x profit:  Rs 7 – 9 m
                 Value at 3x revenue:   Rs 8.6 m
                 Realistic:             Rs 15 – 25 m *

    25 plants    Rs 7.2 m/yr revenue    ~Rs 5.4 m profit
                 Realistic:             Rs 30 – 60 m

    40 plants    Rs 11.5 m/yr revenue   ~Rs 8.6 m profit
                 Realistic:             Rs 60 – 110 m

  * The higher figures assume low churn, documented
    processes and the owner not being indispensable. A
    strategic buyer in the region — someone who already
    sells to plants and wants the product — pays more than
    a financial buyer, because for them it is a product
    line, not a spreadsheet.

  THE POINT

  Ten customers is worth roughly five times the code alone.
  Forty customers is worth twenty to thirty times.

  Every hour spent polishing the product from here returns
  far less than an hour spent getting a plant to sign.
  That is the single most important sentence in this
  document.

  WHAT DESTROYS VALUATION — avoid these

    - Churn above 5% a month. Buyers call it a leaky
      bucket and discount hard.
    - One customer being more than 25% of revenue.
    - Being seen as an "AI wrapper". Your defence is the
      81-table operational system underneath — the AI sits
      on top of real workflow, it is not the whole product.
      Say it that way.
    - Owner dependency. If only you can run it, the buyer
      is buying a job. The master project folder you now
      have is the fix for this, and it is worth real money
      at sale time.
    - Undocumented code. Already solved.
