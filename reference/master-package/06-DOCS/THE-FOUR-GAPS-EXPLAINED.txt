  1. "SOCIAL PREVIEW TAGS" — what they are
===============================================================

  WHAT IT MEANS

  When you paste a link into WhatsApp, the app sends a
  robot to that page a second before you hit send. The
  robot looks for hidden instructions in the page telling
  it three things:

      - what picture to show
      - what headline to show
      - what one-line description to show

  Those hidden instructions are called OG TAGS. "OG" stands
  for Open Graph, which was Facebook's name for it. Every
  chat app now uses the same system — WhatsApp, LinkedIn,
  Facebook, X, Slack, Telegram, iMessage.

  They are invisible. They sit in the page's code, not on
  the page. A visitor never sees them. Only the robot does.

  YOUR PAGES HAD NONE OF THEM.

  So the robot found nothing, and WhatsApp had nothing to
  show. Your link arrived as a bare blue line of text.

  WHY THAT COSTS YOU MONEY

  You sell on WhatsApp. That is your channel. Picture the
  plant manager's screen:

    WITHOUT the tags — what he saw until today
      A grey line of text: hsbfix.org
      No picture. No words. Looks like spam, or a link
      somebody forwarded without thinking. Nothing about
      it says "a real company built this."

    WITH the tags — what he sees now
      A large photograph of a plant floor, your logo,
      the headline "The maintenance app that reads your
      manuals and answers back", and a line explaining
      the AI and the Urdu voice input.

  Same link. Same product. Completely different first
  impression, and the first impression is the whole game
  when you are unknown and asking an industrial buyer to
  trust you.

  This is why I called it the biggest return on the list.
  It is two hours of work and it upgrades every single
  message you will ever send.

  Added the full set of tags to all six pages: index,
  pricing, signup, guide, privacy and terms. Each page has
  its own headline and description, so sharing your pricing
  page shows pricing wording rather than generic homepage
  wording.

  I also built the share image itself — og-image.jpg,
  1200 x 630 pixels, which is the size every platform
  expects. It uses your own hero photograph of the plant
  floor, darkened on the left so white text reads cleanly,
  with your teal brand colour, the HSB logo, your headline,
  and three badges: 19 MODULES, 7-DAY FREE TRIAL, NO CARD

  2. "FAVICON" — what it is
===============================================================

  WHAT IT MEANS

  The tiny square icon in the browser tab, next to the page
  title. Also what shows when someone bookmarks you or adds
  your site to a phone home screen.

  You had none, so every browser showed a blank grey page
  symbol.

  WHY IT MATTERS

  Small, but it is the kind of small that registers
  subconsciously. A plant manager with fourteen tabs open
  sees a blank icon where every real company has a logo.
  It reads as unfinished — a site somebody is still
  building, not a business to hand money to.

  It also means that when he bookmarks you to show his
  boss on Monday, there is nothing to recognise.

  WHAT I DID

  Built your HSB logo as an icon file — near-black rounded
  square, white "HS", teal "B", matching your site header
  exactly. Same colours from your stylesheet: #0d1117 and
  #0f9b8e.

  Four files, because different devices want different
  things:

    favicon.ico           the main one, with six sizes
                          baked inside for every browser
    favicon-32.png        modern browsers
    favicon-16.png        small tab displays
    apple-touch-icon.png  180x180, for iPhone home screens

  3. "THIRD-PARTY REVIEWS" — the one you must do
===============================================================

  WHAT IT MEANS

  Websites where buyers compare software before they buy.
  The big three are G2, Capterra and Software Advice. There
  is also SoftwareSuggest, which is strong in South Asia.

  They are directories. Every CMMS is listed with its
  features, its price, and reviews from real users. When a
  plant manager searches "best maintenance software", these
  directories fill the entire first page of Google — above
  the vendors' own websites.

  MaintainX has 1,540 reviews on G2, rated 4.8.
  Limble has 685. Fiix has 476.

  You have zero. Not a bad score — no listing at all.

  WHY THAT COSTS YOU MONEY

  Here is the part that matters, and it is worse than
  simply "not being rated".

  A buyer comparing options never sees you. He is not
  choosing between MaintainX, Limble and PlantMaster Pro
  and picking one of the other two. He is choosing between
  MaintainX and Limble, because as far as his research is
  concerned, you do not exist.

  You lose those deals without ever knowing they happened.
  No enquiry, no rejection, no feedback. Silence.

  And remember what the comparison would actually show if
  you were on it: Rs 24,000 for a whole plant against
  Rs 450,000 for MaintainX at 25 users. You win that
  comparison overwhelmingly. You are just not in it.

  WHAT TO DO — about 20 minutes each, all free

  Listing yourself as a vendor costs nothing on all of
  these. They make money from advertising and from vendors
  who pay for placement, but a basic listing is free.

    Capterra   capterra.com/vendors
               Biggest reach. Owned by Gartner, feeds
               Software Advice and GetApp too — one listing
               appears on three sites.

    G2         g2.com/products/new
               Most respected by serious buyers.

    SoftwareSuggest  softwaresuggest.com/vendors
               Strong in India and Pakistan. Your market.

  For each: company name, logo, screenshots, a description,
  feature list, and your pricing. You have all of it — pull
  the description straight off your own homepage, it is
  better written than most of theirs.

  THEN THE REVIEWS

  A listing with no reviews is only half useful. When your
  three founding customers are 60 days in and happy, ask
  each for a review. Not a favour — build it into the
  founding-customer deal. You already ask for "two honest
  lines after 90 days" on your pricing page. Ask them to
  put those two lines on Capterra instead of emailing them
  to you.

  Three genuine reviews from named Pakistani plants will do
  more for you than three hundred from anonymous accounts.
  Buyers read the words, not just the stars.

  WHEN TO DO IT

  Do the listings now, this month — they take 20 minutes
  and start building search presence immediately. Get the
  reviews once your founding customers have real experience
  to describe.

  4. "INTEGRATIONS" AND "SAP" — ignore this for now
===============================================================

  WHAT IT MEANS

  An integration is an automatic connection between your
  software and another system the company already runs, so
  data moves without anyone retyping it.

  SAP is the big one. It is enterprise accounting and
  resource planning software used by most large
  manufacturers. Extremely expensive, extremely entrenched.
  In Pakistan the large textile groups, cement plants and
  multinationals run it.

  WHY IT MATTERS — AND WHY IT DOES NOT MATTER YET

  If a company runs SAP, their finance department has a
  rule: every purchase order must exist in SAP. Not in a
  second system. In SAP.

  Your system creates purchase orders. If it cannot push
  them into SAP automatically, someone has to type each one
  twice. Finance will refuse. The deal dies — and it dies
  regardless of how good your AI is or how cheap you are.
  That is what I meant by "SAP kills a deal instantly".

  BUT — and this is the important part — that is a problem
  for companies large enough to run SAP. Those are not your
  first customers. A 25-person plant in Karachi is not
  running SAP. They are running registers, WhatsApp and
  maybe Tally or QuickBooks.

  Your actual market for the next two years does not have
  this problem at all.

  WHAT TO DO

  Nothing. Genuinely nothing, for now.

  You already have a REST API on the Professional plan,
  which means a competent developer can connect anything to
  you if a customer needs it. That is enough of an answer.

  If a prospect asks "does it integrate with SAP?", the
  honest answer is:

     "Not out of the box. We have a REST API, so it can be
      connected — but if SAP integration is essential on
      day one, I would rather tell you now than sell you
      something that disappoints you."

  That answer wins respect and occasionally wins the deal
  anyway, because most people asking do not actually need
  it on day one.

  Build integrations when a paying customer asks and will
  wait for it. Never build one on speculation — you will
  guess wrong about which system, and it is weeks of work.

  Same logic applies to IoT sensors. Your phone-based
  condition monitoring is a feature, not a gap: no hardware
  to buy is the entire selling point. Add sensor input the
  day a customer with sensors wants to pay you.

  CHECK IT WORKED — do this, it takes one minute

  The catch: WhatsApp REMEMBERS. It caches what a link
  looked like the first time it saw it, for about a week.
  So if you send hsbfix.org to yourself now, you may still
  see the old bare link and think it failed.

  Two ways round it:

    a) Send a slightly different address, which WhatsApp
       treats as new:

           https://hsbfix.org/?1

       That should show the picture immediately.

    b) Force Facebook and LinkedIn to re-read the real
       address — this also clears WhatsApp, since they
       share infrastructure:

       Facebook debugger:
         developers.facebook.com/tools/debug/
         Paste https://hsbfix.org and press "Scrape Again"

       LinkedIn inspector:
         linkedin.com/post-inspector/
         Paste the same and click Inspect

    Both are free and neither needs an account beyond a
    normal login. They also show you exactly what each
    platform sees, which is useful for confirming the
    image loaded rather than guessing.

  Then check the favicon: open hsbfix.org and look at the
  browser tab. You want the black square with the teal B.
  If you still see blank, press Ctrl+Shift+R to force a
  reload past the cache.

  ONE MORE THING WORTH DOING

  Once uploaded, submit your sitemap to Google so it finds
  all six pages properly:

      search.google.com/search-console
      Add property > hsbfix.org > verify via DNS or by
      uploading the HTML file it gives you > then
      Sitemaps > enter: sitemap.xml

  Free. Takes ten minutes. It also shows you what people
  searched before landing on your site, which tells you
